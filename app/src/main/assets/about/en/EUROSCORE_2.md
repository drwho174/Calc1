# EuroScore 2

This calculator estimates the predicted risk of perioperative death in patients
undergoing cardiac surgery.

### **Age**
Age in completed years. Of more than 20,000 patients in the EuroSCORE database only 21 were older than 90 years,
so the risk model may be inaccurate for these patients. Please use clinical judgement when interpreting the score.
The oldest patient in the EuroSCORE database was 95 years old.
EuroSCORE II is not validated in patients older than this.

### **Chronic lung disease**
Long-term use of bronchodilators or steroids for lung disease.

### **Poor mobility**
Severe impairment of mobility due to musculoskeletal or neurological dysfunction.

### **Critical preoperative state**
* ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation or aborted sudden death;
* cardiac massage;
* ventilation before arrival in the anaesthetic room;
* inotropes;
* intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation or a ventricular assist device before arrival in the operating theatre;
* acute renal failure (anuria or oliguria <10 mL/h);

### **NYHA classification of dyspnoea:**
* I: no symptoms at moderate exertion
* II: symptoms at moderate exertion
* III: symptoms at mild exertion
* IV: symptoms at rest

### **Angina class IV**
Inability to perform any activity without angina or angina at rest

### **Active endocarditis**
Patients still receiving antibiotics for endocarditis at the time of surgery.

### **Recent myocardial infarction**
Within 90 days before surgery

### **Urgency**
* Elective: elective admission for surgery
* Urgent: patients not electively admitted for surgery
but who require surgery during the current admission for medical reasons
and cannot be discharged without a definitive procedure.
* Emergency: surgery before the start of the next working day after the decision to operate.
* Salvage: patients requiring cardiopulmonary resuscitation (external cardiac massage) on the way to the operating theatre or before induction of anaesthesia.
This does not include cardiopulmonary resuscitation after induction of anaesthesia.

### **Procedure complexity**

* Baseline level — isolated CABG.

Procedures "heavier" than the baseline level fall into three categories:
* Isolated major procedure not related to CABG
(e.g. single valve surgery, ascending aortic replacement, septal defect repair, etc.);
* Two major procedures (e.g. CABG + AVR), or CABG + mitral valve repair (MVR),
or AVR + ascending aortic replacement, or CABG + MAZE procedure, or AVR + MVP, etc.);
* Three or more major procedures (e.g. AVR + MVP + CABG or MVP + CABG + tricuspid annuloplasty, etc.) or aortic root replacement,
if it includes AVR or AVP + coronary artery reimplantation + root and ascending aortic replacement)

Only major cardiac surgical procedures are included in the total.

Examples of procedures that do not qualify are: sternotomy, sternal closure, myocardial biopsy, intra-aortic balloon insertion,
pacing electrodes, aortotomy closure, atriotomy closure;
atrial appendage removal, coronary endarterectomy as part of CABG, etc.


Source: [euroscore.org](https://www.euroscore.org)
