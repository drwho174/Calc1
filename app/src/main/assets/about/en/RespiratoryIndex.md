# Respiratory index and alveolar-arterial gradient

##### _The alveolar-arterial oxygen gradient (pO2(A - a)) is calculated as:_

$$
pO_2(A-a) = (FiO_2 *( AtmPress - H_2OPress ) - (PaCO_2 / 0.8)) - PaO2
$$

_Expected normal gradient = (Age / 4) + 4_

###### CLASSIFICATION OF HYPOXIA BY THE A-a GRADIENT

Normal A-a gradient

1. Alveolar hypoventilation (raised PaCO2)
2. Low PiO2 (FiO2 < 0.21 or barometric pressure < 760 mmHg)

Elevated A-a gradient

1. Diffusion defect (rare)
2. V/Q mismatch
3. Right-to-left shunt (intrapulmonary or intracardiac)
4. Increased oxygen consumption (CaO2-CvO2)

##### _The respiratory index is calculated by the following formula:_

$$
RI = pO_2(A-a) / PaO_2
$$

Normal RI < 0.4. Rarely used.

##### _P/F ratio_

Also known as the Horowitz index, the Carrico index and (most conveniently) the P/F ratio.
This ratio is calculated as

$$
P/F = PaO_2 * 100/ FiO_2 
$$

At sea level the normal P/F ratio is ~400-500 mmHg.

##### _S/F ratio_

Can be considered as an alternative to the P/F ratio for a faster diagnosis of impaired pulmonary oxygenation.
It has certain limitations described in the article below
[Rationale and limitations of the SpO2/FiO2 as a possible substitute for PaO2/FiO2 in different preclinical and clinical scenarios](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9345592/#r4)
